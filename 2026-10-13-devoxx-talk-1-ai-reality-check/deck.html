A trust ladder in seven stages

Stages 1-5 - PermissionOne agent, more rights. How much do I approve?
1Browser chat

Copy-paste by hand, both ways. Low trust, total control.

2Approval

A coding agent; you approve every tool call.

3Collaborator

File edits go through; shell commands still approved.

4Director

High trust: you judge the end result, not each step.

5YOLO mode

Full trust, fast autonomous iteration.

Stages 6-7 - ShapeMany agents. Who splits the work?
6Orchestrator

You coordinate a fleet of specialised subagents.

7Architect

You design systems that design systems.

1Flyway migration script

Stage 2: every change approved by hand before it runs against a real schema.

2Unit tests, Maven gate

Stage 4: agent writes freely, a required CI gate judges the result, not each step.

3Shared library refactor

Stage 3: edits go through automatically, but any shell command still needs a yes.

  • System design stays human: you can't vibe your infrastructure

Key takeaways

  1. 1A feeling is not a measurement: judge AI by lead time, failure rate and rework, not by how fast it feels.
  2. 2Trust is transferred, not decreed: hand over control only where a test, a build or CI watches for you.
  3. 3The brain is the bottleneck, not the keyboard: climbing the ladder spends judgment, protect it.
